Worked Examples: Solving Real Problems

Here are common problems people bring to a calcsolver, worked through step by step so you can follow the method and check your own answer.

How to  calculate a percentage of a number

Problem: What is 15% of 240?

Convert the percentage to a decimal by dividing by 100, then multiply:

15 ÷ 100 = 0.15
0.15 × 240 = 36

So 15% of 240 is 36. The Percentage Calculator does this in one step, including the reverse case (what percent is 36 of 240).

How to calculate a discount

Problem: A $60 jacket is 20% off. What do you pay?

Discount: 60 × 0.20 = 12
Final price: 60 − 12 = $48

A faster route is to multiply by what you still pay: 100% − 20% = 80%, so 60 × 0.80 = $48 directly.

How to add fractions with different denominators

Problem: 3/8 + 5/12

Find the lowest common denominator. 8 and 12 both divide into 24, so convert both fractions:

3/8 = 9/24  (multiply top and bottom by 3)
5/12 = 10/24  (multiply top and bottom by 2)
9/24 + 10/24 = 19/24

19 and 24 share no common factor, so 19/24 is the simplified answer. The Fraction Calculator shows this working visually.

How to solve a linear equation

Problem: Solve 3x + 5 = 20

Isolate x by undoing each operation on both sides:

3x + 5 = 20
3x = 20 − 5 = 15  (subtract 5 from both sides)
x = 15 ÷ 3 = 5

Check it by substituting back: 3(5) + 5 = 20. ✓ The Equation Solver shows every step for equations like this.

How to use sin, cos and tan

Problem: Find sin(45°)

Type 45, then press sin. Make sure the calculator is in degrees, not radians:

sin(45°) = 0.7071  (exactly √2 ÷ 2)

This is the single most common trig mistake: sin(45) in radian mode returns 0.8509, a completely different angle. Check your mode before trusting a trig answer.

How to calculate powers and roots

Problem: What is 210, and what is √144?

2^10 = 1024
√144 = 12  (because 12 × 12 = 144)

In Code Mode you can type these directly as 2^10 and sqrt(144) rather than clicking buttons.

How to find the mean of a set of numbers

Problem: Find the mean of 12, 15, 18, 20, and 25.

Sum: 12 + 15 + 18 + 20 + 25 = 90
Count: 5 values
Mean: 90 ÷ 5 = 18

The Statistics Calculator also returns the median and mode for the same data set.

How to work out a tip

Problem: An 18% tip on a $47.50 bill.

Tip: 47.50 × 0.18 = 8.55
Total: 47.50 + 8.55 = $56.05

For a rough mental estimate, 10% of the bill is $4.75, half of that is about $2.38, and adding them gives roughly $7.13 for 15%.

Every Calculation Runs in Your Browser, Not on a Server

Most people never think about where a calc solver's  math actually happens. On a lot of calculator sites, typing an expression sends it off to a server somewhere, waits for a response, and prints whatever comes back.

You don't notice this until the server is slow, or down, or your school's WiFi throttles the request - and suddenly a simple square root takes three seconds.

SolveCalc (or solve calc, as plenty of people type it) doesn't work that way. Every calculation - basic arithmetic, trig, logarithms, fraction simplification, the graphing calculator plotting your equation - runs entirely in JavaScript, inside your own browser, on your own device.

Nothing you type gets sent anywhere. There's no round trip, no queue, no "server busy" message. The answer appears the instant you press Enter because there was never anywhere else for it to go.

This matters for two practical reasons beyond speed. First, privacy: whatever you're calculating - a grade, a budget, a homework answer - never leaves your device, isn't logged, and isn't tied to an account, because there's no account and no backend collecting it.

Second, reliability: once the page has loaded, the calculator keeps working even on a flaky connection, since it isn't waiting on anything else to respond. That's also why it holds up fine on older school Chromebooks and slower networks where server-dependent tools visibly lag.
